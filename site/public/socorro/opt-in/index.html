Opt-in Process

Drew Fustin: Socorro household texts
Effective November 20, 2026

Socorro is an automated AI assistant application operated by Drew Fustin. It texts only members of Drew Fustin's household who have opted in, as described here.

1. Verbal consent, in person

Drew Fustin asks each household member in person, using this script:

"Want texts from Socorro, our family assistant? She'll send reminders, schedule changes, and answers to your questions. Message frequency varies, and message and data rates may apply. Reply STOP anytime to stop, or HELP for help. The terms are at drewfustin.com/socorro/terms. If you want them, text her number to start."

No one is signed up without saying yes.

2. How the number is given

For each person who says yes, Drew Fustin gives them Socorro's number in person and saves it in their phone's contacts under the name "Socorro". The number is not advertised or published anywhere.

3. The person texts first

Socorro never texts anyone first. Each person opts in by sending Socorro a text themselves. Socorro's first reply confirms it:

Socorro (Drew Fustin's family assistant): you're signed up for household texts. Msg frequency varies. Msg & data rates may apply. Reply HELP for help, STOP to opt out.

4. How consent is recorded

Socorro's software keeps a consent record for every household member: an entry when they opt in (with their name, mobile number, the date and time of their opting-in text, and the message ID of that text and of the confirmation), and an entry whenever they reply STOP or START. Socorro can only text household members whose latest record is an opt-in. Records are kept privately; the fields look like this:

EventNameMobileDate and timeHow
opted in(household member)(their mobile) (time of their first text)first text; confirmation sent
opted out(household member)(their mobile) (time of their STOP)replied STOP

5. Opting out

Anyone can reply STOP at any time; they get one confirmation and no more texts. START opts back in, and HELP gets help.
